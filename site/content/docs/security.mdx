<PlainWords>

Turn is new, and nobody has checked its code independently yet. We say so everywhere, and during the beta we cap how much a circle can hold.

Here's what Turn is designed to guarantee: nobody, not us and not the organiser, can take a circle's money outside its rules, and members who pay on time always get their full turn.

This page lists what could go wrong, how we test for it, and what we haven't solved yet.

</PlainWords>

## Audit status

**Unaudited hackathon build.** No external audit has been done. Mainnet use is capped, with `maxContribution` defaulting to 25 AUSD and `maxMembers` to 10. Please use small amounts.

## Threat model

| Actor | Could try to | Why it doesn't work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A member** | Take the pot early and stop paying | Collateral covering their remaining debt is withheld from the payout. Any waived part is backed by the reserve (I1). |
| **A member** | Snipe or signal in the auction | Bids are sealed with commit–reveal. The salt comes from a key only they hold. |
| **A member** | Block a round by not paying | `collect` retries each member separately, and `markDefault` covers them after grace. Payout is delayed by at most `gracePeriod`. |
| **The organiser** | Move the pot | The organiser has no special role after creating the circle. |
| **The relayer** | Steal or alter transactions | Every batch is EIP-712 signed by the member (nonce, deadline, chain ID). The relayer can only **censor**, which any member with MON can route around. |
| **The keeper** | Pay the wrong person, or skip rounds | It can only call permissionless functions that check their own rules. The worst case is a late round, and anyone can call them. |
| **The factory owner** | Freeze funds | `pause` only blocks new circles and joins. `collect`, `payout`, `markDefault`, `claim` and withdrawals can never be paused. |
| **Someone else's contract** | Pull from a member's account | `TurnAccount` pulls only for factory-registered circles, within the grant's amount, round and expiry. |
| **Front-running** | Reorder bids or payments | Monad has no public mempool. Commit–reveal makes ordering irrelevant for bids. |

## Invariants and testing

The invariants I0–I3 are defined on [Safety](/docs/safety#the-invariants).

**Done:**
- The circle economics are ported to TypeScript (`site/lib/economics`) and tested with Vitest:
  - `plan.md`'s worked ejection example, to the unit
  - shared vectors for `TrustMath` and for auction scenarios
  - **400 randomized circles** (3–10 members, random amounts, bids, trust, reserve shares and missed payments), asserting I0, I1 and I3 after every step and I2 at the end
- Those tests already found and fixed spec gaps:
  - leftover reserve could get stuck when every member had missed once
  - large bids needed a cap so the payout can always cover the withheld collateral
  - an ejection in the final round turns that round into the settlement slot
  - collateral must round **up** on release

**Planned for the contracts (Foundry):**
- unit tests for each contract
- fuzz tests over bids, amounts, timings and trust inputs
- **invariant tests** for I0–I3 with a handler that performs random joins, payments, misses, bids and time jumps
- the same `vectors.json` run against Solidity, so the two implementations can't drift apart
- scenario tests: the seven required flows, ejection settlement, and a trusted member's default covered by the reserve

## Known limitations

- **Unaudited**, as above.
- **The factory owner is a single deployer key.** Moving it to a multisig is future work. The owner still can't touch funds.
- **Relayer censorship.** A censoring relayer can delay users who have no MON. Round progress doesn't depend on it.
- **Reveal liveness.** A sealed bid that isn't revealed in time doesn't count.
- **Fixed-order circles** build almost no reserve, so trust rarely waives collateral there.
- **Exchange-rate risk.** The pot is dollars. Its value in rupees or dirhams moves.
- **Stablecoin risk.** Turn depends on AUSD holding its peg and staying transferable.
- **Local rules.** Savings committees are regulated differently in different places. Turn is a tool for groups who know each other.
- **Fresh-device sign-in** without a stored credential is still being verified with mera.
